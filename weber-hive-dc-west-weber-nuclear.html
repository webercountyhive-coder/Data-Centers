West Weber: Zoned for Data Centers, Now Talking Nuclear

In 2023, Weber County made data centers a permitted use on land in what became the West Weber inland port area. In 2025, a state law let big power users build their own power plants. In 2026, the county began public talks about nuclear power for the port. Here is what the records show, in order.

The short version

  • In 2023, landowners asked Weber County to add data centers to the uses allowed in the new M-T (Manufacturing and Technology) zone in western Weber County. The county approved it 3-0. Nobody spoke against it at either hearing.
  • In 2025, S.B. 132 let any customer using 100 megawatts or more get power from its own private plant if Rocky Mountain Power can't serve it in time or on agreed terms.
  • In August 2026, the county and the state energy office held a public symposium on nuclear power, framed around the port's need for electricity.
  • No data center or reactor has been proposed in West Weber. The county says no sites or projects have been discussed. The pieces that would allow one are already in place.

What was said in August

The Aug. 5 symposium was co-hosted by Weber County and the Utah Office of Energy Development. KUER, KSL, ABC4 and the Standard-Examiner covered it.

  • Why the county held it. Economic Development Director Stephanie Russell told KUER the port area needs stable jobs, the companies that bring them need electricity, and nuclear is one option. She said the goal was to "dispel myths, but we also want to talk about pros and cons."
  • What the county says is not happening. County spokeswoman Jessika Clark told KSL no site locations or projects have been discussed. Commissioner Gage Froerer called it "just an option."
  • Where nuclear could fit, per the county. Clark named the West Weber project area and the county's proposed Industrial District and Renewable Energy Hub. The county's brochure for that hub describes a 6,000-plus-acre site, served by Union Pacific rail, about 7 miles west of I-15.
  • What the panel covered. Siting factors including earthquakes, flooding and water supply. Cambrian Nuclear CEO Ethan Bates said water for cooling limits today's reactors, while some smaller designs are air-cooled.
  • Who spoke. BYU professor Matthew Memmott gave the keynote. Panelists were Michael Squires, Chris Hayter, Ethan Bates, John Battaglini and Michelle Zietlow-Miller.
  • The next step. KUER reports a listening session in December or January, where residents can weigh in on the county's zoning and development policies.

Already here: a nuclear-fuel supplier, not a power plant

Westinghouse has a plant inside the port project area that makes zirconium and hafnium used in nuclear fuel. That is manufacturing. There is no reactor in Weber County.

The county brochure, read as written

The Renewable Energy Hub brochure lists Stephanie Russell as its contact. It projects 25,000 new jobs, 4,000 housing units and $780 million in new state revenue over 20 years. It also lists "25,000 sq. ft." of industrial and commercial space for a 6,000-plus-acre site. That may be a typo for a larger number. It is reported here as printed.

Update, Oct. 24, 2026: what the Inland Port Authority told lawmakers

At an Oct. 21, 2026 legislative committee hearing, UIPA Executive Director Ben Hart said: "At this juncture we are not actively recruiting data centers to the state." For comparison, UIPA's own May 2025 report listed data centers among West Weber's recruitment prospects, and Weber County made data centers a permitted use there in 2023. Who would get the power?

This is the question the records don't answer yet. It matters because of how S.B. 132 works.

  • Under S.B. 132, a "closed private generation system" serves only its customers, stays separate from the utility's wires, and is exempt from regulation as a public utility.
  • Elsewhere in Utah, the documented nuclear plans so far are tied to specific data center campuses. See Utah's nuclear projects →
  • For West Weber, no record reviewed says whether a plant would serve the grid or only port tenants. That is an open question.

What this page does not say

  • Who applied for the 2023 data center change. The minutes say only "land owners" of the M-T property. The December 2022 packet that may name them is a scanned PDF that could not be read as text, so it is listed as unread, not empty. PCC is not named as the applicant here.
  • That nuclear at West Weber is for data centers. No West Weber record ties the two. The county's stated reason is jobs and electricity for port companies.
  • That the 355 M-T acres and PCC Land's 376.86 acres are the same land. They are near each other. Matching them takes parcel numbers.

The other side

  • Education came first. Weber County held its public symposium before any project was proposed, the reverse of how Box Elder County's Stratos data center came to light. Russell told KUER that if leaders don't hear from residents, they'll assume everyone is fine with it, and that the county wants to know whether it's a good fit or not.
  • Ratepayer protection is real. S.B. 132 requires large users to pay their own costs, and the Public Service Commission may approve a utility contract only if existing customers don't bear them. Utah's Office of Consumer Services called it one of the best protections in the nation.
  • The zoning change was noticed and heard. Both the Planning Commission and the County Commission held public hearings in 2023. No one spoke against it.
  • The area has long been zoned for industry. County staff noted in 2024 that much of western Weber County has been zoned industrial for years, alongside Compass Minerals and Westinghouse.

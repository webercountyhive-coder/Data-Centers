Eagle Mountain, Utah County · Referendum Docket

The City Won't Hand Over the Petition — And the Pamphlet Has a Date State Law May Not Allow

Sponsors of a second referendum against Eagle Mountain's 69.81% property-tax increase say the city is withholding the signature-gathering materials it's legally required to provide, and that its draft ballot pamphlet lists an August 2027 special-election date that Utah election law doesn't appear to permit for a tax measure.

Referability
A city attorney's legal determination that a proposed referendum is allowed to go before voters under Utah Code Title 20A, Ch. 7 — a procedural green light, not a ruling on the outcome.
VPA
Voter Participation Area — the geographic unit Utah uses to organize by-mail ballot counts and precinct-level boundaries.
Truth in Taxation
Utah's required public-hearing process before a city can adopt a property-tax rate above its certified (revenue-neutral) rate.
Certified Rate
The property-tax rate that would raise the same total revenue as last year, before new growth — the baseline any increase is measured against.
New Here? The Short Version

Eagle Mountain's City Council raised its property-tax rate 69.81% on August 18, 2026, to help fund a Utah County Sheriff's Office contract. A group of residents is trying to force a public vote to repeal it — their second such attempt this cycle. The city's own attorney says the referendum is legally allowed to proceed. But sponsors say the city still hasn't handed over the petition forms, signature sheets, or an accurate ballot pamphlet it's required to provide within five days of that determination — and that the draft pamphlet lists an August 2027 election date for a tax measure that Utah law appears to only allow in November.

2. The Date Problem

Sponsors' central legal objection to the draft ballot pamphlet is its proposed election date: August 10, 2027. Utah election law appears to foreclose that date for a tax-related ballot measure absent a specific, documented exception — and nothing in the public record reviewed for this piece shows that exception was invoked.

What the statute says

Utah Code § 20A-1-204(1)(d) "The legislative body of a local political subdivision may only call a special election for a ballot proposition related to a bond, debt, leeway, levy, or tax on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November."

That date is fixed by statute, not by local choice: November 3, 2026 this year, November 2, 2027 next year. An August date for a tax referendum is not one of the options the statute lists.

The only exception

Utah Code § 20A-1-204(1)(c) A local legislative body may call a special election on a different date only if it: (A) determines and declares that a disaster, as defined in § 53-2a-102, requires a different election date; (B) identifies the specific nature of the disaster and the reasons for the different date; and (C) votes unanimously to hold the election on that date.

No step in this chain has a documented "yes" in the public record reviewed for this piece — which is why the pamphlet's Aug. 10, 2027 date lands in the red box above.

Finding No declared disaster, and no ordinance or resolution invoking this exception, appears in the public record reviewed for this piece. Utah Code § 20A-1-203(5)(a)(iv) and (b) additionally require the city council to call any special election by ordinance or resolution stating the date and purpose — a formal legislative act, not a pamphlet drafting choice.

In other words: if Eagle Mountain has not adopted such an ordinance or resolution, an August 10, 2027 date on the pamphlet did not originate from a council vote at all — which is consistent with sponsors' account that the date was inserted during drafting, not adopted by the council. That distinction — a drafting choice versus a legislative act — is the crux of the dispute, and it is independently checkable against the city's own council minutes and any special-election ordinance, which this piece has not yet obtained.

3. The Materials Fight

Under Utah Code § 20A-7-613(7), once a referendum is determined referable, the City Recorder must provide the referendum petition, signature sheets, and an accurate Proposition Information Pamphlet within five calendar days. Sponsors say that hasn't happened, and that what has been provided is defective.

4. Other Spending Sponsors Point To

The sponsors' public "Argument in Favor" cites several other city expenditures as context for why they see the tax increase as avoidable. Some of this checks out against independent, primary-source records; one claim could not be verified at all.

Confirmed

  • MHTN Architects feasibility study. A June 16, 2026 council meeting agenda lists both "MHTN Architects to Conduct a City Hall & Library Feasibility Study" and an action item on "compensation increases for executive municipal officers" on the same day — corroborating sponsors' claim that the city heard both items at once. This piece did not independently confirm the specific $56,500 payment or the 5.49% raise figure sponsors cite; a prior year's public notice shows a different cap (5.9%, FY2025-2026), so the two years' figures should not be conflated.
  • $220 million water/sewer bond. A public notice confirms the City Council authorized Water and Sewer Revenue Bonds, Series 2026, "not to exceed two hundred twenty million dollars," approved Feb. 17, 2026 with a March 5, 2026 public hearing. Sponsors' materials cite $228 million — an $8 million discrepancy from the official authorization figure that hasn't been resolved.

Unverified

Could not confirm Sponsors' materials reference a mayoral claim that the city is leaving the Unified Fire Service Area for roughly $4 million/year in savings. No public record found in this piece's research supports Eagle Mountain leaving UFSA — the city's own government page still describes Unified Fire Authority as its current fire/EMS provider in the present tense. This claim should not be treated as established without a direct primary source (a council statement, agenda item, or minutes) confirming it.

Questions Worth Asking

  • Ask the Eagle Mountain City Recorder's office: has the referendum petition, signature sheets, and pamphlet been transmitted to the sponsors, and on what date?
  • Ask the City Council: has any ordinance or resolution been adopted declaring a disaster and unanimously setting an August 2027 election date under § 20A-1-204(1)(c)? If not, on what authority does the pamphlet cite that date?
  • Ask the City Attorney's office: for a copy of the September 14, 2026 referability determination letter itself.
  • Ask the Utah County Clerk's elections office: whether any Eagle Mountain referendum has been submitted for a future ballot, and on what date it would appear.
  • Ask your state senator (Sen. Emily Buss, District 11): whether the Legislature plans to clarify when a local tax referendum may be voted on.
  • Ask the City: for the specific VPA boundary documents provided to sponsors, and the date they were last updated.

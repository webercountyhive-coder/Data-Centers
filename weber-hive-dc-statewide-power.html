It's Happening Statewide: Utah's Data Centers and Their Own Power Plants

Two-thirds of the data center capacity under construction in Utah is planned to run behind the meter or off the public grid. Region by region, the same three pieces keep showing up: local zoning, a 2025 state law, and nuclear as the long-term power source.

The short version

  • Utah's biggest new data centers are being built with their own power plants, mostly natural gas for now.
  • A 2025 law, S.B. 132, lets any customer using 100 megawatts or more do that when Rocky Mountain Power can't serve it in time or on agreed terms.
  • Nuclear is offered as the long-term source in several regions, and in the documented cases it is tied to specific campuses.
  • The law keeps those costs off other customers' bills. It also means other customers don't receive that power.

The numbers

Source: Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute, "Data Center Growth in Utah and the United States," April 2026. The grid figure is the remainder of 2,600 minus 1,700, rounded.

  • Where Utah's data centers are today: 48 operating, about 920 MW. The ten largest make up nearly 80% of that.
  • Under construction: seven projects, about 2,600 MW. Joule's campus near Delta is the biggest at 1,400 MW for its current phase.
  • Grid warning: the fact sheet says the North American Electric Reliability Corporation projects elevated grid risk for Utah in 2031, when supply may fall short under extreme conditions.
  • Proposed, at full planned size: the Salt Lake Tribune's September map lists about 65 sites, with campuses in Box Elder, Millard, Duchesne, Iron, Carbon and Utah counties listed at a gigawatt or more.

Why the pieces line up: the utility can't keep up

  • Rocky Mountain Power's only firm reactor plan is TerraPower's plant in Kemmerer, Wyoming, around 2032. Utah would share its roughly 500 MW with other Western states.
  • Developers say they can't wait. Google delayed an Eagle Mountain campus over power supply. Joule's managing partner told reporters the company is excited about nuclear someday but can't wait for it, so it is building gas power.
  • S.B. 132 is the path. If the utility can't finish its study or agree on terms in time, a large customer may contract with a private generator. A fully off-grid "closed private generation system" is exempt from regulation as a public utility (§54-26-504).

Read this fairly

Promoting energy development is the Office of Energy Development's job under Operation Gigawatt. The point here is a narrower one for readers: the office that runs the public "education" events is also a partner in specific projects. That's worth knowing when you sit in one of its sessions.

What this page does not say

  • That anyone coordinated these projects. The record shows the same law, the same state office and some of the same people. It does not show a plan.
  • That every proposed data center will be built. Several are proposals only; Tooele's is paused.
  • That nuclear will be built at any data center. In Utah, so far, only a test reactor is operating.

The other side

  • Ratepayers are protected from these costs. S.B. 132 requires large users to pay all their own costs. Michele Beck of Utah's Office of Consumer Services called it one of the best ideas out there for protecting customers.
  • Off-grid plants don't compete for grid power. A campus making its own power isn't drawing on supply that homes and businesses use.
  • Rural counties want the tax base. Millard and Carbon officials point to lost coal jobs and new tax revenue. Carbon County coal mines have produced none since 2023.
  • The policy is public. Operation Gigawatt's goal of doubling power production is stated openly by the governor.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Utah Public Service Commission
S.B. 132 requires the commission to review the program before Oct. 31, 2027, with public comment. When will that review open, and how can residents comment?
Your state legislators
Should private plants serving one campus have to report their fuel, emissions and output publicly, the way utilities do?
Utah Office of Energy Development
Which of the companies at your public nuclear events are also partners on state-supported projects?
Rocky Mountain Power
How many large-load requests under S.B. 132 has the utility received, and how many went to private generators?
Your county commission
Are data centers a permitted use anywhere in our county, and do they need a public hearing?

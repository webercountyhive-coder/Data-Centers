H.B. 76 (2026): The First Law Requiring Data Centers to Report Their Water

Starting in 2026, new large data centers in Utah must tell the state how much water they expect to use and how much they actually take, and the state must post each one's numbers online. The law covers only data centers that open on or after July 1, 2026. None of the Utah data centers with public water numbers today fall under it.

The short version

  • H.B. 76 requires new large data centers to report their water: expected use before construction, actual withdrawals every year after.
  • The state's Division of Water Rights must post each covered center's yearly withdrawals online, by name, not lumped together.
  • It applies only to data centers that start operating on or after July 1, 2026, that use 75 acre-feet a year or more, and that cover at least 10,000 square feet.
  • The seven Utah data centers with water numbers in public reporting all opened before that date, so the law doesn't reach them.

One case in point: Meta's monthly water use is confidential

Meta's 2018 agreement with Eagle Mountain keeps the campus's monthly water use confidential, so the only public number is the yearly total Meta reports itself. H.B. 76 would not change that for buildings already operating. Meta case file →

Stratos: reporting written into a draft agreement, with a confidentiality clause

Stratos's draft interlocal agreement with Box Elder County requires MIDA to report energy, jobs and other figures to the county each year, the reporting tied to H.B. 76. The same section says the information may be marked confidential, and the county must treat it that way. That agreement is posted as an unsigned draft. Separately, Stratos filed a water rights change application in October 2026 claiming a closed recycling loop with 90% recovery. Stratos pages →

Open questions this page does not answer

  • Expansions. Whether a new building at a campus that opened before July 1, 2026, such as Meta's or QTS's in Eagle Mountain, counts as a "new large data center" is not settled in the records reviewed.
  • What counts as protected. The bill lets some reported information be treated as protected and published only in a county summary. Which items fall in that category is not listed on this page.
  • First postings. No covered data center would have a full year of data until 2027, so the first posted numbers should come in 2027 or later.

The other side

  • It adds rules written for data centers. Covered centers' numbers get posted by name, not just in county totals.
  • The threshold is low enough to matter. At 75 acre-feet, four of the seven centers with public numbers would have qualified if they were new.
  • It asks for plans, not just totals. Covered centers must describe water reuse, discharge treatment and efforts to cut use.

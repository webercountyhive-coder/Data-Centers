"Zero Water" in Eagle Mountain, in plain words

Posted Oct. 28, 2026

What this is

Someone commented online that the new data centers in Eagle Mountain don't lose any water, because they cool their computers with a "closed loop," like a car radiator. We checked that against news reports and state records.

Who's involved

  • QTS, a company building three big data center buildings in Eagle Mountain right now.
  • Meta (Facebook's company), which already runs a data center in Eagle Mountain.
  • Novva, a data center in West Jordan that already uses a closed loop.
  • Rep. Jill Koford, who sponsored Utah's new data center water-reporting law, H.B. 76.

What the records show

The closed-loop part is true for QTS. QTS says its cooling system only has to be filled with water once, and then the same water goes around and around. That's like a car radiator. This is what the company says. Nobody outside the company has published measurements yet.

It isn't true for every data center in Eagle Mountain. Meta's data center there took more than 35 million gallons of water in 2024. A 2018 agreement with the city keeps its monthly water numbers private.

"Zero water" only means the cooling loop. The QTS buildings still use city water for the first fill, bathrooms, kitchens, landscaping and keeping the air from getting too dry. And making electricity takes water too. Researchers told KUTV that the power plants are usually the bigger water user. Closed loops need more electricity than the older kind of cooling.

The comment is right that this is better than the old way. Novva's owner says his West Jordan data center uses about 3 million gallons a year with a closed loop. He says the older evaporating kind would have used about 250 million.

What isn't known yet

  • How much water QTS's first fill took. The company hasn't said.
  • How much water QTS will use in a year. Utah's new law, H.B. 76, makes data centers that open after July 1, 2026 report their water, but only if they use 75 acre-feet (about 24.4 million gallons) or more a year. If QTS uses less, it won't have to report.
  • Meta's monthly water use. Meta opened before the law, so it doesn't have to report.

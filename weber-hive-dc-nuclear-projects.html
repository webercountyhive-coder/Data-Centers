Utah's Nuclear Projects and the Data Centers Behind Them

Utah has one test reactor running, one state land lease signed for a reactor campus, and at least seven more nuclear plans or proposals. Here is where each stands, and what the public record says about who would use the power.

Posted Oct. 24, 2026 · Updated Oct. 24, 2026

The short version

  • Most of Utah's nuclear plans are private projects, not plans by the state's main utility to add power to the public grid.
  • In five of the ten projects below, a data center is named in the record as a planned user of the power.
  • The utility serving most Utahns, Rocky Mountain Power, has one firm reactor plan: TerraPower's plant in Kemmerer, Wyoming, around 2032, shared with other states.
  • Several of these projects sit on public land or use public tools: a state-owned lab, state trust land, a federal land application, and MIDA's development standards.

The Carbon County lease, in the state's own numbers

Trust Lands staff presented these terms at the board's Aug. 20, 2026 meeting. The board approved the lease unanimously on Sept. 17. Item: SULA 2095 OBA, Section 16, Township 14 South, Range 11 East, about 6 miles north of Wellington.

TermAs presented
Land480 acres now, plus 160 acres after the Utah National Guard relocates
Appraised value$750 an acre ($480,000 for 640 acres)
Upfront bonus$400,000
Yearly rent per acre$30 in years 1–5, $206 in year 6, $419 in year 7, $625 from year 8, with inflation adjustments after year 10
Length50 years plus two 25-year renewals (up to 100 years)
Rent over 50 yearsAbout $35 million, to the school trust
Purchase optionValar may buy the land. Staff called a purchase option rare for a surface lease. Conservative buyout value: about $6 million.
WaterThe trust commits no water
WasteNo spent fuel or waste storage unless Valar buys that portion; reclamation and federal decommissioning bonds required

Two records disagree on one number

What the land earns today from the National Guard: the meeting transcript records staff saying about $4,000 a year; the written minutes say about $4,000 a month. Both are reported here until the board's records settle it.

Waste storage: two descriptions

Trust Lands staff said in August there would be no spent fuel or waste storage on the state parcel unless Valar buys that portion. An October news report, citing NPR, described the larger campus as including a nuclear fuel facility and waste storage. The larger campus includes federal land the state lease does not cover.

What this page does not say

  • The size of the Carbon campus. NPR reported documents describing 456 small reactors and 9.6 gigawatts. That figure has not been checked against the federal application and is not used as fact here.
  • Who buys Brigham City's power. No record reviewed names a customer.
  • Claims about a Carbon County farm and a grazing lease. A circulating post said a family faced eminent domain and another lost a grazing lease. No public record reviewed confirms either. No speaker at the Aug. 20 Trust Lands meeting mentioned a grazing lessee.
  • That Stratos will build a reactor. Nuclear is permitted in its standards. That is not a proposal. MIDA's development agreement and standards are posted as drafts and are unsigned.

The other side

  • Water. Valar's reactor is cooled by helium in a closed loop, and the company says its Emery data center would use almost no water. Holtec's SMR-300 can use air-cooled condensers, which matters in a dry state.
  • Schools. The Carbon lease is projected to pay about $35 million in rent to the school trust over 50 years, on land that earns little today.
  • Local support. Emery County's commission sent Valar a letter of support. All three Carbon County commissioners spoke in favor of the lease.
  • Ratepayers. Under S.B. 132, a private plant serving one campus does not put its costs on other electric customers.
  • Public goal. Gov. Cox's Operation Gigawatt openly aims to double Utah's power production in ten years. None of this is hidden policy.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Trust Lands Administration
Does the National Guard currently pay about $4,000 a year or a month for its part of the Wellington parcel? Will the signed SULA 2095 lease be posted?
Bureau of Land Management, Utah
When will Valar's Carbon County application be complete, and when will the public comment period open?
Utah Office of Energy Development
Who are the expected customers for the Brigham City and Green River reactors: the public grid, or specific companies?
Carbon County Commission
Has the county signed nondisclosure agreements with Valar or Altigen? Will any be released?
Rocky Mountain Power
Besides TerraPower in Wyoming, does the utility have any plan to buy nuclear power from a Utah project?

Updates since this was posted

  • State regulator: H.B. 78 (2026), Nuclear Regulatory Amendments (primary sponsor Rep. Carl Albrecht, Senate sponsor Sen. Derrin Owens), created a Nuclear Energy Regulatory Office inside the Division of Waste Management and Radiation Control. It serves as the state's main contact with the NRC on fuel-cycle licensing. Effective May 6, 2026. (Enrolled bill)
  • Valar's money: Valar announced a $1 billion Series B round led by Sequoia Capital on Aug. 4, 2026, plus a $200 million credit facility, to build manufacturing, fuel production and reactor fleets. (Pulse 2.0; Goodwin; Latham & Watkins)
  • More companies at the state lab: the San Rafael Energy Lab's own page also lists Natura Resources (2026) and NuCube Energy (2025, a memorandum of understanding) besides Valar.
  • Carbon County: Valar's Carbon plan is called "Project Beehive." Trust Lands board chair Mike Nelson said at the Sept. 17 meeting, "We weren't always sure what to ask about." (Canary Media, Oct. 14, 2026)
  • See also: the Hive's Transparency page One Reactor, Two Regulators covers the federal reactor rule and Utah's test reactor in more depth.

This page covers only the ten companies whose complete Utah records the Hive has pulled. QTS, which is building a $6 billion Eagle Mountain campus, was also searched and has no Utah political contributions on record, and neither does Kevin O'Leary under his own name. Giving money is legal, and nothing here shows any vote was traded for it.

What changed in the last three years

  • More money. These ten gave about $186,000 over the six years from 2018 to 2023, and about $583,000 in the three years from 2024 to 2026.
  • Creekstone gave to the leadership PACs. Creekstone Energy, which is building the Delta data center campus, gave $2,000 each to the Utah House Republican Election Committee and the House Speaker Leadership PAC on Aug. 28, 2025, then $1,000 each to Rep. Walt Brooks and the Utah Republican Senate Campaign Committee in 2026. The state announced Creekstone's incentive, up to $172 million in tax credits over 20 years, in April 2026.
  • Amazon gave once. Amazon's only Utah political giving on record is from 2019: $5,000 to then-Attorney General Sean Reyes and $330 to the Santa Clara Community PAC. Every other entry under Amazon's name, 2018 to 2026, is a store refund or return.
  • Oracle gave to two statewide officials. Oracle, which runs data centers in Salt Lake City and West Jordan, gave $40,000 from 2020 to 2026: $25,000 to Gov. Spencer Cox, $10,000 to Attorney General Derek Brown in 2025 (filed as two same-day $5,000 entries that may be one check entered twice), and $5,000 to the Utah County Legislative PAC in 2026.
  • Google arrived in 2026. From 2018 through 2025, Google's counted giving in Utah was about $1,100, mostly small checks from Google Fiber. In 2026 it gave $65,500 in 30 checks, nearly all to Republican legislators and both Republican leadership PACs, including $5,000 to Sen. Kirk Cullimore, S.B. 114's primary sponsor, and $5,000 each to the House and Senate Republican campaign committees. One 2026 check went to a Democrat, Rep. Doug Owens.
  • Fewer, bigger checks. In 2018–2020, Micron gave about 100 small checks a year, mostly $200 to $1,000, to legislators in both parties, then stopped after 2021. Since 2022, Torus has given $50,000 checks, every one to Gov. Cox. Meta's first Utah spending was $251,446 in one day, on two primary races.
  • Outside spending appeared. Meta's money went to ads, calls, mailers and data for Tami Tran (Senate District 6) and Logan Monson (House District 69) rather than to their campaigns, so it does not appear in either candidate's own report. Both won their June 23 primaries.
  • Steady giving continued alongside it. Lumen, which runs four data centers in Salt Lake City and Ogden, has given $10,000 to $34,000 a year since 2021, mostly to both parties' leadership PACs and Gov. Cox.

  • Dan Hemmert. HEMMCO's state lobbyist registration lists MIDA as a client from Jan. 19, 2023 to Aug. 3, 2026, and most of his other clients ending on Aug. 3, 2026. MIDA's board appointed him executive director (Resolution 2026-12); his start date was reported as Aug. 1, 2026. MIDA approved the Stratos project area on April 24, 2026, before he was hired. Hemmert's LinkedIn lists Meta as a HEMMCO client. As a state senator, he was a member of the Senate committee that passed S.B. 114 in 2020 and was absent for that vote.
  • Derek Brown. The state lobbyist registry lists Derek Brown as a lobbyist for Facebook from Dec. 6, 2017 to Dec. 2, 2023, with his license canceled Jan. 3, 2024. That period includes Facebook's 2018 Eagle Mountain data center approvals and the 2020 passage of S.B. 114. Brown was elected Attorney General in 2024. The registry shows dates and clients only; it does not show what issues a lobbyist worked on.
  • Lincoln Hill Partners. Its 2021 merger announcement named four principals: Lincoln Shurtz, Casey Hill, Chase Everton and Derek Brown, who is now Utah's Attorney General. Everton has been registered for Meta since 2017. Shurtz lobbies for MIDA, according to the Salt Lake Tribune, and Shurtz and Hill were both registered for O'Leary Ventures in spring 2026, around the time MIDA approved Stratos. Hill is also registered for Creekstone Energy. HEMMCO's registration lists Lincoln Hill Partners as a client from Feb. 1, 2023 to Aug. 3, 2026.
  • Google's lobbyists. Austin Cox, whom the Salt Lake Tribune identified as Gov. Cox's 2020 campaign manager, was registered for Google from Aug. 12 to Oct. 9, 2021. Spencer Stokes and Matt Holton, both registered for Google since 2022, share names with the two members listed for Roost Communications LLC in Utah business records; a Spencer Stokes is also listed with Casey Hill as an officer of Gathering, Inc. Matching names alone do not confirm they are the same people.
  • Gov. Spencer Cox. Received all of Torus's contributions, Dell's one contribution and $24,000 from Lumen.
  • Meta's Utah PAC is chaired by Josh Althouse, Meta's public policy director; its treasurer is an attorney at a Fremont, California political law firm.

The other side

  • Every contribution and expenditure on this page was reported to the state and is public. That is how the Hive found it.
  • Companies of every kind give to Utah campaigns. Lumen and Micron gave to Democrats and Republicans alike.
  • Torus makes energy storage for utilities, data centers and industry. A company giving to a sitting governor is legal, and the records don't say why it gives.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Your state legislators
Have you received money from data center companies or the companies that power them, and did it come before or after votes on data center bills?
Gov. Spencer Cox's campaign
Every Torus contribution on record, $201,042 since 2022, has gone to your campaign. Does Torus have any contract, grant or matter before the state, and how is that handled?
Lt. Governor's Office (elections)
Should outside spending like Meta's show up on the candidate's own disclosure page, so voters see it in one place?
Candidates in 2026 races
Will you list on your website every company that spent money to help you, including outside spending?
